The Maths Behind Spirographs

Parametric equations, gear geometry and symmetry principles

What is a Spirograph Curve?

A mathematical spirograph models the trajectory traced out by a fixed pen point attached to a rolling wheel moving without slipping along the circumference of a stationary circular ring.

Hypotrochoid (Inside)

Created when the smaller wheel rolls along the inside perimeter of the fixed ring radius \(R\).

Epitrochoid (Outside)

Created when the smaller wheel rolls along the outside exterior boundary of fixed ring radius \(R\).

Parametric Maths Equations

Given fixed ring radius R, rolling wheel radius r, pen distance from wheel centre d, and rotation angle t:

Hypotrochoid Equations:

x(t) = (R - r) · cos(t) + d · cos((R - r) · t / r)

y(t) = (R - r) · sin(t) - d · sin((R - r) · t / r)

Epitrochoid Equations:

x(t) = (R + r) · cos(t) - d · cos((R + r) · t / r)

y(t) = (R + r) · sin(t) - d · sin((R + r) · t / r)

Symmetry & Petal Calculation (GCD)

1. Simplify the fraction R / r using its Greatest Common Divisor GCD(R, r) to a / b.

2. The simplified numerator a yields total petals/lobes.

3. The simplified denominator b indicates the full rotations needed to close the loop.
