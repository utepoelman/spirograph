The Maths Behind Spirographs

Parametric equations, non-slip kinematics & geometry in NZ English

1. The Non-Slip Rolling Condition

When a smaller wheel of radius r rolls along a fixed outer ring gear of radius R without slipping, the length of the arc traversed along the fixed ring must exactly equal the length of the arc rolled along the wheel boundary.

Arc Length Equivalence Equation:

s = R · θ = r · φ

Where θ is the orbital angle around the main centre, and φ is the total rotation angle of the rolling wheel.

Rearranging gives the rotation of the inner wheel relative to its own centre: φ = (R / r) · θ.

2. Vector Derivation of Pen Coordinates (x, y)

The pen position is found by vector addition: adding the vector from the origin to the centre of the rolling wheel, plus the vector from the wheel centre to the pen hole at distance d.

Hypotrochoid (Wheel Inside Fixed Ring):

Wheel Centre: C = ((R - r)cosθ, (R - r)sinθ)

x(θ) = (R - r) · cos(θ) + d · cos(((R - r) / r) · θ)

y(θ) = (R - r) · sin(θ) - d · sin(((R - r) / r) · θ)

Epitrochoid (Wheel Outside Fixed Ring):

Wheel Centre: C = ((R + r)cosθ, (R + r)sinθ)

x(θ) = (R + r) · cos(θ) - d · cos(((R + r) / r) · θ)

y(θ) = (R + r) · sin(θ) - d · sin(((R + r) / r) · θ)

3. Extended Geometries (Pen Hole Outside Wheel d > r)

In a physical plastic spirograph kit, the pen hole must lie inside the physical boundaries of the wheel (d ≤ r). In our mathematical studio, setting d > r simulates an extension arm extending outside the physical disc! This yields dramatic self-intersecting loops, starbursts, and wild trochoids impossible with standard toy sets.

4. Symmetry & Great Common Divisor (GCD)

The curve seamlessly closes back onto its starting point when the ratio R / r simplifies to the irreducible fraction a / b:

• Total Lobes / Petals = Simplified Numerator a

• Full Rotations Needed to Close Curve = Simplified Denominator b

• Calculated via GCD(R, r)
